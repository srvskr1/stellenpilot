Job hunting in Germany without fluent German

By Sourav Sarker · 22 October 2026

You can find work in Germany without fluent German, but not every job is open to you, and the ads do not always say so clearly. Spend your time on jobs where German is not the deciding factor, and be specific about the level you have.

Where English works

English is most common in software, data, research, startups and international companies, and in teams where colleagues come from many countries. Bigger cities have more of these jobs, but they exist across the country, including the Ruhr area.

German matters most where you talk to German customers, patients, authorities or suppliers every day: sales, support, healthcare, public sector and many small companies. Even with excellent skills these roles are hard without at least B2 German.

How to read the language line in an ad

  • An ad written fully in English is the strongest sign that the team works in English.
  • "Deutschkenntnisse von Vorteil" or "wünschenswert" means nice to have. "Fließend", "verhandlungssicher" or "zwingend erforderlich" means it is required. Our guide on what "verhandlungssicher" really means goes through the common phrases.
  • If the tasks mention clients, Kunden or Behörden, expect German even when the ad is in English.

Where to search

  • Search with English job titles (Data Engineer, Working Student, Analyst). English titles pull in more English ads.
  • Add the word "English" to your search, or look for an English language filter where the job board offers one.
  • Check the career pages of international companies near you directly. Many of their jobs never reach the big boards.
  • Read German ads anyway. A translation tool is enough to see whether German is required or only nice to have, and some German ads turn out to be open to English speakers.

Present your German honestly

Write your level as a CEFR level, for example German B1, and add how it is moving: "B2 course until March 2027". A clear level that is growing reads better than a vague word like "basic" or "conversational", and it shows you plan to stay.

Do not round up. Interviewers sometimes switch to German for a few minutes, and a CV that said fluent then works against you. With B1 on your CV, a simple conversation is exactly what they expected.

Which language to apply in

Apply in the language of the ad. If the ad is in German but you are not confident writing German, a short English application with one line saying you are improving your German is better than a German letter full of mistakes. When unsure, ask the contact person in the ad which language they prefer.

Plan your next step in German

Many ads that require German ask for B2 or higher, so moving from B1 to B2 can open a lot of them. Keep a simple list of the jobs you skipped because of German. After a few weeks the list shows you whether the next step in German or another technical skill would open more doors for you.
