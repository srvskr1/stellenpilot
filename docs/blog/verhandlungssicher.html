What "verhandlungssicher" really means in a job ad

By Sourav Sarker · 22 October 2026

"Verhandlungssicher" appears in a lot of German job ads. It is not an official language level, and employers use it in slightly different ways.

The literal meaning

Verhandlungssicher means "safe in negotiations". You can lead a business conversation, discuss contracts or complex problems, and explain your position without mistakes that change the meaning. In practice most employers read it as C1 or higher on the CEFR scale.

Common phrases and what they usually mean

None of these words are defined by law or by the CEFR. Employers use them loosely, so treat this table as a common reading, not a rule.

Grundkenntnissebasic knowledgeA2 to B1
gute Deutschkenntnissegood GermanB1 to B2

Spoken, written, required or optional

  • "In Wort und Schrift" means spoken and written. Expect to write emails and documents in German, not only to talk.
  • "Zwingend", "setzen wir voraus" and "erforderlich" mean required. "Von Vorteil", "wünschenswert", "idealerweise" and "ein Plus" mean nice to have. Read this part as carefully as the level.

Should you apply below the level?

If the job is client facing and the ad says verhandlungssicher and zwingend, applying with B1 rarely works. The requirement is real because the work happens in German.

It is different when the rest of the ad is in English, the team is international, or German appears only as a nice to have. Then a strong technical match can outweigh the language line. If you are unsure, write to the contact person and ask how much German the team uses day to day.

On your own CV

Only call yourself verhandlungssicher if you would be comfortable negotiating your salary in German. Otherwise give the CEFR level and the certificate, for example German B2 (Goethe Zertifikat). That matches what the interviewer will hear.
